Oak Puzzle — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 16 November 2026

This page is published at Oak Puzzle's privacy website.

Oak Puzzle is a single-player puzzle game published by ATROPIA ("we"). This policy explains what the game does with information.

Summary

Oak Puzzle has no accounts of its own and no analytics, and it keeps your progress and profile on your device. If your phone's Android backup is on, Android also keeps a copy in your own Google Drive, so your progress returns on a new phone. The game shows ads through Google AdMob. To show them, Google may use your device's advertising ID. The one thing you send out is your leaderboard scores, if you use the leaderboards. The Ads and Leaderboards sections explain both.

Information stored on your device

The game saves the following on your device so that it can continue where you left off:

  • your best score;
  • the levels you have unlocked, the leaves and best score of each level, and the world you selected;
  • how many games you have played, how many lines you have cleared and your longest combo;
  • in Mahjong: the levels you have unlocked, and the stars, best time and best score of each level;
  • in Elixir Sort: the levels you have unlocked, the stars, best time and fewest moves of each level, the bottle style you chose, and an unfinished level so you can continue it;
  • in Wordora: the levels you have unlocked and the words you have found;
  • your coins in each game, which you earn by winning levels and spend on helpers;
  • your settings (language, sound, background music and its volume, theme, simple effects, the daily reminder and its hour);
  • the day's three tasks, how far you have come with them, your streak and your leaf shields;
  • the name and score of yesterday's leaderboard winner, so that the daily screen can show them;
  • your profile, if you fill it in: the name you choose, your age, gender and country (all optional), the avatar you pick, and, if you choose one, a copy of the photo you use as your profile picture.

We cannot see this information. It is removed from the phone when you clear the app's data or uninstall the game.

Android backup

If your phone's backup setting is on, Android copies this data to a private folder in your own Google Drive. Your progress then returns when you install the game again or set up a new phone.

Android does this, not us:

  • the copy belongs to your Google Account, and we cannot read it;
  • on Android 9 and later it is encrypted with your screen lock;
  • other apps cannot read it either;
  • it uses no space in your Google Drive;
  • only the newest copy is kept, and Android deletes it after a long time without use.

You can turn the backup off in your phone's settings, under System and then Backup.

To choose the language it starts in, the game reads your phone's language setting; this happens on the device and is not stored anywhere else. You can change the language in Settings.

Information we do not collect

The game has no accounts of its own, no analytics and no tracking of our own. The leaderboards use the Google Play Games profile you already have; we never see your e-mail address or anything else in it. It does not read your contacts, location, files, microphone or camera. It does not guess your country from your location. We do not receive your advertising ID. Google AdMob uses it, as the Ads section says. It sees a photo only if you pick one yourself as your profile picture, through your phone's own photo picker: it then receives that one photo and keeps a copy on your device. We never receive it. That copy is included in the Android backup described above, if you have the backup on. No permission to your photo library is requested.

Ads

The game shows ads from Google AdMob, in two ways:

  • Videos you choose to watch. You can watch a short video for extra coins, or to double the coins from a level.
  • Ads between levels. Now and then an ad appears after you finish a level. It never appears during play.

To show an ad, Google AdMob may collect and use your device's advertising ID, your IP address, and information about your device and about how the ad performed. Google may use this to choose and measure ads. Google does this under its own policy: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. We do not receive this information.

  • Your choice. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and some other places, the game shows Google's consent message the first time it opens. Where you can change your answer, Settings shows "Ad privacy choices".
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings, under Google or Privacy. You can also turn off ad personalisation there.
  • Content. The game asks for ads suitable for a general audience.
  • On your device. The game saves only how many videos you have watched today and when ads last appeared, so that it can keep ads rare. This never leaves your device.

Leaderboards

The game has leaderboards, run by Google Play Games Services. They are the only part of the game that publishes anything.

  • Only with a Play Games profile. If you already have one, Google Play Games signs you in by itself, and your score is sent when you finish a game that a board ranks. If you have none, the game never asks you to make one, and nothing is sent.
  • What is sent. Three numbers and nothing else: your daily challenge score, your best Endless score, and how many leaves you have earned. Google shows them with your Play Games name, which other players can see. That name comes from your Play Games profile, not from the name you type in the game.
  • What comes back. The twenty best names and scores of the board you are looking at, which the game shows you.
  • Who keeps it. Google keeps the board under its own privacy policy. We run no server, we never see your e-mail address, and we cannot read anything else in your Play Games profile.
  • Your choice. The Google Play Games app lets you change the name other players see, hide your profile, or delete what it holds for this game. The rest of Oak Puzzle plays the same either way.

Permissions

  • Internet access — the game needs it to load ads, for the leaderboards, and for the update check below. Without a connection, the game works, and no ads appear.
  • Advertising ID — lets Google AdMob show and measure ads, as described above.
  • Network state and wake lock — added by the ads software, so that it can check for a connection and play videos.
  • Change audio settings — lets the game play its sounds and music alongside other apps' audio instead of interrupting it.
  • Notifications — only if you turn the daily reminder on. Android also lets the game set its reminders again after the phone restarts.

The game asks for no other permissions. The links in the game (the full privacy policy and "Rate the game") open in your browser or in Google Play.

Reminders

The daily reminder is off until you turn it on in Settings, and you choose the hour. It is written by your own phone: nothing is sent to us or to anyone else, and the game needs no connection for it. At most one reminder arrives a day, and never at night. Turning the switch off removes every reminder the game had planned.

Updates

On Android, the game asks Google Play on your phone whether a newer version is available (Google Play's in-app updates). If one is, Google Play shows its own update dialog. Google Play handles the check and the download under its own privacy policy. The game sends nothing to us and learns only whether to offer the update. On your device it saves only the version you said no to and when, so that it does not ask again for a few days.

Third parties

The game includes two Google services: Google AdMob, for ads (see Ads), and Google Play Games Services, for the leaderboards (see Leaderboards). It has no analytics software. Android's own backup service, part of your phone, stores the copy described above under Google's privacy policy. If you install the game from Google Play, Google processes data about the download and about crashes under its own privacy policy; we receive only aggregated, non-identifying statistics from Google Play.

Age

Oak Puzzle is intended for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. We do not collect personal information from anyone. Ads are requested for a general audience.

Changes

If the game changes in a way that affects privacy, this policy will be updated and the effective date above will change.
